2. Website Troubleshooting & Free Tech Insights

The Technical Cause: Every time a visitor clicks a page on WordPress, the server executes dozens of PHP files and queries a MySQL database. Over months and years, post revisions, spam transients, and uninstalled plugin tables bloat your database. Add 20+ plugins each loading their own CSS/JS files, and your server's CPU maxes out.

The Solution: Regularly prune your database transients and revisions, disable unused plugin assets from loading globally, or convert to pure static HTML so no database queries are needed at all.

The Technical Cause: WordPress core, PHP, themes, and plugins are built by hundreds of different authors. When you click "Update All", a newer plugin might call a function deprecated in your server's PHP version, or conflict with your page builder hook, causing a fatal PHP error (the infamous "White Screen of Death").

Practical Tip: Never click "Update All" on a live production server. Always run off-site backups first, or test plugin updates on a staging subdomain before applying them to your live website.
